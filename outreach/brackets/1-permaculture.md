# Bracket 1 — Permaculture and water-harvesting practice

**~12 contacts, mostly tracker Tier 1.** National bodies (UK, Australia, NZ, USA),
design networks, education networks, the rainwater-harvesting standards body, and
three well-known educators.

## Who
Designers and teachers who site earthworks for a living, plus the organisations
that train and represent them. Global: Australia, USA, UK, NZ.

## What they measure
Whether water stays on the property longer. Where a swale actually goes if it is
to stay on contour. Where the keypoint is. Whether the dam will hold. They decide
these with a level, a walk, and experience — and they are usually right, which is
the point to respect.

## What they distrust
Software that treats a landscape as a machine to be optimised. Engineering jargon.
Subscription pricing. Being told what land needs by someone who has not walked it.

## Facts that prove it, in order
1. `TF-62` **the origin story.** A rewilding site that lost several years of
   plantings to drought is the exact thing this audience has watched happen. Lead
   here, not with the tool.
2. `TF-27` keyline: keypoints, ridgelines, pond sites, cultivation lines. Their
   vocabulary, implemented.
3. `TF-23` sizing feedback and how overflow cascades from one structure to the
   next — the cascade is the thing experience gets wrong most often.
4. `TF-21` in-built identification of suitable earthwork locations.
5. `TF-10` public LiDAR where it exists, coarser open elevation data anywhere it
   does not. **Never lead with LINZ here** — nine of these twelve are not in NZ.

## Opener mechanism — the judgement call
Name the moment where experience carries the whole decision, without implying
their judgement is wrong. The tool shows the water; it does not know the land.

> Siting a swale on contour is a judgement call right up until someone digs it.

> You can read a keypoint off the ground if you have walked enough valleys. I
> wanted to see whether an open DEM could find the same ones.

## Limits to state
`TF-70`, framed as: it shows where water concentrates and what an earthwork would
change. It does not know your soil, your history, or what you are trying to grow.

## The ask
Half an hour, and specifically to hear where it is wrong about a place they know
well. That invites expertise rather than adoption.

## The three educators — Lawton, Millison, Lancaster
**Same ask as everyone else.** Feedback before release, half an hour, written
feedback if a call does not suit. Do not ask for coverage, a share, or their
audience — see `guardrails.md` rule 5. Frame the tool in terms of what their
audience does, and let them decide the rest.

Two tracker notes bind: Lawton's personal address came from a lookup service and
must not be used — the organisation address is the one on file. Lancaster's site
says he rarely answers unsolicited mail, so treat it as a long shot.

## Never say
- "Optimise" anything. Not the word, not the idea.
- That it designs a system. `TF-27` identifies features; the design is theirs.
- Yield, productivity or efficiency claims. There is no basis for any.
- Anything implying keyline is a technique he invented or improved.
- LINZ, unless the recipient is in New Zealand.
