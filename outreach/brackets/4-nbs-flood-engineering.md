# Bracket 4 — NbS, SuDS and flood engineering

**~8 contacts, tracker Tiers 3 and 5.** Arup, Mott MacDonald, Royal HaskoningDHV,
AECOM, Stantec, the Nature-based Solutions Initiative (Oxford), CIRIA / susdrain,
and the OPW in Ireland.

## Who
Global consultancies and the UK/Irish institutional hubs for nature-based
solutions and sustainable drainage. Mostly reached through a general enquiry form
rather than a person, which changes the odds but not the content.

## What they measure
Whether a method is defensible when someone senior asks how the number was
produced. Time from elevation data to a position they can hold in a meeting.
Whether it fits the stack they already run rather than adding a licence and a
training day.

## What they distrust
Black boxes. Unvalidated tools presented with confidence. Anything that would be
awkward in a peer review. A stranger implying their existing modelling is
inadequate.

## Facts that prove it, in order
1. `TF-70` **lead with the limit.** The gap between "we should look at this" and
   "we have modelled it" is where this sits, and naming it precisely is the pitch.
   To this reader the caveat is the credential.
2. `TF-30`–`TF-33` CAMELS-NZ and the WhiteboxTools match — with the one-catchment
   qualifier, always. An engineer who spots an unqualified validation claim stops
   reading, and this bracket will spot it.
3. `TF-40` D-infinity by default, D8 available, and why.
4. `TF-41` SCS Curve Number. `TF-42` the Manning equation for diversion drains.
   Named, standard, checkable methods — no novelty claimed.
5. `TF-52` QGIS 3.22+ LTR. `TF-51` coverage on the calculation layer, said as the
   calculation layer.

## Opener mechanism — the early-stage gap
Do not open on capability. Open on the position in the workflow.

> Between "we should look at this" and "we have modelled it" there is a fortnight
> where the answer is a sketch and a feeling.

> Before a site is worth a full model build, someone still has to decide whether
> the idea is worth modelling at all.

## Ireland — OPW
Liam is Irish, and the OPW is one of three Irish contacts. That is a legitimate
opener when it is true and specific, not a claim of connection. It belongs in the
tailored opener, cited `owner`, never as flattery.

## Limits to state — mandatory
`TF-70` **and** `TF-71`. It informs early-stage judgement, it does not replace
detailed hydraulic modelling, and one catchment has been validated rather than the
tool. Omitting either is the fastest way to be dismissed by exactly the person
most worth hearing from.

## The ask
Half an hour, and specifically: where would this fall over in front of a reviewer.
Ask for the objection, not the endorsement.

## Never say
- That it compares to, competes with, or replaces SWMM, HEC-HMS, TUFLOW or
  InfoWorks. `TF-70` says the opposite and they will know.
- "Validated", "accurate", "verified", "industry standard", "production ready".
- Anything implying their current process is slow or wrong.
- A number without an `[f:]` behind it. This bracket checks.
