# Bracket 2 — Restoration and watershed NGOs

**~13 contacts, tracker Tiers 2 and 4.** Global: Netherlands, India, Kenya, USA,
Ireland, Switzerland, Germany, Sri Lanka. Restoration coalitions, watershed
development NGOs, reforestation developers, conservation bodies, development
agencies.

## Who
Organisations restoring degraded land at scale, usually with more sites than
budget, and usually accountable to a funder for what changed.

## What they measure
Where to intervene first, given a season only holds so many plants and so much
volunteer or contractor time. Whether an intervention did anything. What they can
put in front of a funder next year that is not a photograph.

## What they distrust
Consultant fees. Tools priced for engineering firms. Greenwash. Technology
brought to them by someone who has not stood on a degraded site.

## Facts that prove it, in order
1. `TF-62` **the origin story — this bracket's single strongest asset.** A
   rewilding project that lost several years of plantings to drought is precisely
   what these organisations are trying to stop happening. It is not a marketing
   line, it is the reason the tool exists, and it is why this bracket should hear
   from him before anyone else.
2. `TF-20` and `TF-24` run the analysis, draw the intervention, re-run it, and see
   what changed before anything is dug.
3. `TF-10` open elevation data anywhere. **This is what makes the tool usable in
   Kenya, Maharashtra, Tanzania and Sri Lanka**, where LiDAR does not exist. Say
   it explicitly — most of this bracket assumes a tool like this cannot work on
   their sites.
4. `TF-02` free and open source, permanently. No licence line in a grant budget.
5. `TF-26` ponding volume in litres.

## Opener mechanism — the sequencing problem
Their constraint is order, not ambition.

> A season only holds so many plants, so the question is never what to do, it is
> what to do first.

> Everyone can see where the water ends up. The harder question is which part of
> the catchment is sending it there.

## Limits to state
`TF-70`, and `TF-33` wherever validation is mentioned. For a group that reports to
funders, the qualifier is what makes the rest of the email trustworthy rather than
a pitch.

## The ask
Half an hour, and whether it would survive being shown to a funder or a field
team.

## Never say
- Any carbon, biodiversity or ecological outcome claim. It models surface water.
- That it will help them win funding. It might help them show their reasoning.
- "Rewilding" to an organisation that does not use the word about itself — check
  the tracker's *Why It's a Fit*. Most say restoration, revegetation or watershed
  development.
- Anything about indigenous or community land practice unless their own material
  frames it that way, quoted.
- LINZ. None of this bracket is in New Zealand.
